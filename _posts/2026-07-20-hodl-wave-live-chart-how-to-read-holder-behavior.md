A HODL wave is a stacked chart that shows what share of Bitcoin supply last moved within each age band, from days to over ten years. Warm colors mark recently moved coins and cool colors mark long-dormant ones. Thickening cool bands point to accumulation. A sudden reset of old bands points to distribution.

You can view the chart live, and you can read it in four steps. It tracks holder behavior, not price, which is why it often shows you what the price chart hides. This guide covers where to find a live version, how to read it, and the pitfalls that distort it.

## What Is a HODL Wave and How Does It Work?

A HODL wave groups every Bitcoin in existence by how long it has sat still, then stacks those groups into one chart that always covers the full supply. You are not looking at price. You are looking at patience.

The chart rests on two terms. Bitcoin tracks ownership as unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs), which work like individual bills in a wallet: each one is created when coins arrive and destroyed when they are spent. Coin age is the time since a given UTXO last moved. When coins move, the old UTXO is spent, a new one is created, and its age resets to zero.

That reset drives the whole chart. A coin that sits untouched for five years climbs through the age bands. A coin that moves today drops into the youngest band, no matter how long it was held before.

The name comes from "hodl," a misspelling of "hold" in a 2013 Bitcoin forum post that became a community byword for refusing to sell. Some readers expand it as "hold on for dear life," but that came later. The term means holding through volatility.

Analysts use coin age as a proxy for conviction. Coins that have not moved in years belong to holders who sat through multiple price swings. Coins that move often belong to traders. The chart shows the share of supply in each camp and how that share shifts over time.

Long-term holder supply is a common cut of the same data. CoinGlass notes that it is typically calculated using a threshold of approximately 155 days of dormancy.

If two sources disagree by a point or two, that is normal. Providers treat exchange-held and lost coins differently.

## Step 1: Categorize Bitcoin Supply Into Age Bands

Start by learning what each band represents, because every later reading depends on it. Providers slice supply by time since last movement, and the slices usually run from under a day through one week, one month, three months, six months, one year, two years, and on to ten years and up.

Each band is a stripe in the stack. The bottom or top stripe holds coins that moved in the last day, and stripes further along hold progressively older coins. Because the chart always covers the full supply, a thickening stripe means that age group now holds a larger share of supply.

Where providers draw their lines matters, and the lines are conventions, not laws of nature. The long-term holder cutoff is a good example: CoinGlass notes that long-term holder supply is typically calculated using a threshold of approximately 155 days of dormancy. Some charts use a flat one-year line instead. Neither is wrong. Check the legend before you compare two charts.

The oldest bands deserve special attention. Maketo reports that as of November 2026, the 10 years and up band represents 17.9% of all Bitcoin supply. Coins that old are often assumed lost or permanently held. They rarely move, so they anchor the bottom of the chart.

To finish this step, open one of the charts from the previous section and write down the band boundaries it uses. Note the youngest band, the one-year line, and the oldest band. You will use that map to read colors in Step 2.

## Step 2: Read the Colors for Market Sentiment

The colors do the sentiment work for you. Warm tones (reds, oranges, yellows) mark coins that moved recently. Cool tones (blues, greens, purples) mark coins that have sat still for a long time. You can get a first impression without reading a single number.

A chart dominated by warm bands describes a market where coins change hands fast. That happens during rallies and sell-offs, when traders rotate in and out and long-dormant holders get tempted. A chart dominated by cool bands describes a market where most supply is parked, which is what quiet accumulation and long bear-market stretches look like.

Watch the boundary between the two, not just the totals. The border where warm turns to cool separates traders from holders. When it moves toward the cool side, older coins are being spent. When it moves toward the warm side, the youngest coins are aging into longer bands.

Colors cut both ways. A thick warm stripe can mean fresh buying interest, or it can mean old holders cashing out. The color tells you coins moved. It does not tell you why.

For this step, pull up your chart and set it to the longest range available. Mark where the cool bands were thickest and where the warm bands bulged. Then compare those periods with price. Warm bulges usually cluster around the loud moments, and cool stretches around the quiet ones.

## Steps 3 and 4: Spot Accumulation, Supply Tightness and Distribution Resets

Now you read direction. Step 3 is to watch the older bands expand. Step 4 is to watch them collapse.

**Step 3: observe band expansion for supply tightness.** When cool bands thicken over weeks and months, coins are aging instead of moving. Fewer coins are available to sell, so supply tightens. This is accumulation: holders are adding, not spending. The pattern builds slowly, and a single week of change tells you little.

**Step 4: watch for supply resets indicating distribution.** When old bands shrink sharply and warm bands swell at the same time, long-held coins have moved. That is a reset, and it often marks distribution, where long-term holders sell into strength. Accumulation takes months. Distribution can show up in days.

The one-year line is the checkpoint most analysts watch. CryptoSlate reports that, according to Maketo data, the percentage of Bitcoin supply that has not moved for at least one year reached 63.3% on September 18, 2026. Treat that as one source on one date, not a forecast. A high share of year-old coins means a lot of supply is locked up. It does not mean price must rise.

Patience has paid in the past. Unchained, a vendor, states that the average historical return for holding bitcoin for two years is just under 25x. That is a backward-looking vendor figure, and it does not promise the same result next cycle.

Look for both signals together. Cool bands expanding plus a calm warm band is tightening supply. Cool bands collapsing plus a swollen warm band is a reset worth checking against other data before you conclude anything.

## How to Combine HODL Waves With Other Metrics and Avoid False Signals

A HODL wave is a context tool, not a trading signal on its own. It cannot predict a top or a bottom. Use it to frame a question, then confirm the answer elsewhere.

The biggest trap is the custodial reshuffle. When an exchange or custodian migrates coins between wallets, the old UTXOs are spent and new ones are created. Coin age resets to zero even though nobody sold anything. On the chart, that looks like a sudden collapse of old bands, which reads as distribution. It is bookkeeping.

Before you call an old-band drop a sell-off, ask whether coins moved between wallets owned by the same entity. Entity-adjusted data, which groups addresses belonging to one owner, filters most of that noise.

Use this checklist and table to confirm what you see:

| HODL wave signal | What to confirm with another metric |
|---|---|
| Old bands thickening | Entity-adjusted supply and exchange balances falling |
| Sharp reset of old bands | Entity-adjusted flows to exchanges, to rule out an internal wallet migration |
| High one-year-plus share | Price and volume context, since locked supply does not guarantee a rally |
| Warm bands swelling | Whether the movement is new buying or old coins being spent |

Risk-adjusted returns add a second layer. The Sharpe ratio measures return per unit of volatility. Bitcoin Data Guide reports a Sharpe ratio of 0.78 for the 2020-2025 window. Algoros data indicates that since August 2012, the median one-year Sharpe ratio has been 1.39. The windows differ, so do not compare the two numbers directly. Use each as context for how much volatility holders absorbed during that period.

Check at least two independent sources before you act on any reading.

## FAQs

### What is HODL short for?

HODL is not an acronym. It started as a misspelling of "hold" and became shorthand for refusing to sell through price swings. Today it describes long-term holding as a strategy and a habit.

### How many BTC are left in circulation?

Bitcoin's supply has a fixed cap, and most of it has already been mined. The remaining coins are released through block rewards on a schedule that shrinks over time. Check a live supply tracker for the current figure, since it changes with every block. Some coins are also lost permanently, which lowers the effective circulating amount.

### What is the Sharpe ratio of BTC?

It depends on the window you measure. Bitcoin Data Guide reports a Sharpe ratio of 0.78 for the 2020-2025 window. Algoros data indicates that since August 2012, the median one-year Sharpe ratio has been 1.39. The two cover different periods, so treat them as separate context, not a range.

### What is the difference between a HODL wave and a Realized Cap HODL wave?

A standard HODL wave weights each age band by the number of coins. A Realized Cap HODL wave weights each band by the price at which those coins last moved, so it reflects the capital invested at each age instead of raw coin counts. Glassnode Studio offers both views. Use the second one when you want to see where dollars sit, not just coins.

### Can a HODL wave predict a Bitcoin top or bottom?

No. It describes holder behavior and gives you context, but it is not a trading signal on its own. A high share of old coins does not guarantee a rally, and a reset does not guarantee a drop. Use it to frame a question, then confirm with entity-adjusted flows and price context.

## Conclusion

A HODL wave shows you who is holding and who is selling by grouping Bitcoin supply by age. Cool bands thickening point to accumulation, a sharp reset of old bands points to possible distribution, and a custodial reshuffle can fake that second signal.

Before you act on any reading, open one live chart and note where the one-year line sits. Then check whether an old-band drop shows up in entity-adjusted exchange flows, not just coin age. If it doesn't, treat it as bookkeeping, not selling.
