Not every crypto analysis app does the same thing. Some decode blockchain data at a granular level. Others focus on stock screening. A few have added AI layers that let you ask questions in plain language. And at least one now schedules agents to deliver research to you automatically.

If you're managing both a crypto and a US equity portfolio, the right platform depends on what you're actually trying to do. This comparison breaks down seven platforms by their genuine strengths so you can match the tool to the job.

## The Core Problem With Most Research Stacks in 2026

Most active retail investors end up running three or four tools in parallel. On-chain signals for Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana live in one place. Stock data lives somewhere else. News aggregation is a third tab. Pulling it all together every morning costs real time — and during volatile markets, like a Bitcoin breakout or earnings season, slow research is expensive.

The platforms below each solve part of that problem. Knowing where each one starts and stops is what makes the difference.

## Glassnode: Best for Deep On-Chain Crypto Metrics

Glassnode is the standard reference for on-chain data depth. It covers more than 1,700 metrics across more than 1,500 assets, with over 15 years of historical data. If you want to track miner flows, exchange reserves, long-term holder behavior, or SOPR on Bitcoin, the data is there.

The tradeoff is audience and interface. Glassnode is built for institutional analysts and developers who are comfortable reading raw charts and building their own interpretive layer. There's no agent automation, no scheduled research delivery, and no US equities coverage. You pull the data; you do the analysis.

**Best for:** Institutional-grade on-chain research, primarily on Bitcoin and Ethereum.

**Not built for:** Retail investors who want completed insights, anyone tracking US stocks, or users who want proactive research delivery.

## Nansen: Best for Wallet Intelligence and Smart Money Tracking

Nansen's core strength is labeled wallet data — more than 500 million labeled addresses, which means you can track where institutional wallets, exchange wallets, and historically profitable addresses are moving funds. That kind of smart-money signal is genuinely useful for understanding positioning before price moves.

Like Glassnode, Nansen is crypto-only and oriented toward institutional users. No US equity coverage, no no-code agent automation, no scheduled delivery. It requires active use and interpretation.

**Best for:** Tracking on-chain wallet flows and smart money positioning across crypto.

**Not built for:** Retail investors who want equity data alongside crypto, or anyone who wants research delivered on a schedule.

## Messari: Best for Protocol Research and Crypto Fundamentals

Messari sits closer to the research analyst end of the spectrum. It covers protocol economics, token metrics, governance data, and broad API access for developers. If you want to understand the fundamentals of a specific Layer 1 or DeFi protocol, Messari has the structured data and written research to support that.

The platform isn't agentic. There's no scheduled automation, no AI agent framework, and no equity market coverage. You navigate to the research; the research doesn't come to you.

**Best for:** Deep protocol-level research on crypto projects, particularly for DeFi-native users.

**Not built for:** Investors who want proactive insights, US stock coverage, or a mobile-first experience.

## AInvest: Best for AI-Assisted Stock Research With a Low Entry Cost

AInvest takes a different angle. Its AIME engine covers stock screening, news summarization, and portfolio analysis through a conversational interface. There's a free tier and a paid plan at $9.99 per month, and the platform has built a large Android user base.

The limitation is scope. AInvest is primarily stock-focused — on-chain crypto intelligence isn't a highlighted capability. It also operates on a query-response model, meaning you initiate the research. It doesn't deliver proactive analysis on a schedule.

**Best for:** Retail investors who want AI-assisted stock research at an accessible price point.

**Not built for:** On-chain crypto analysis, scheduled autonomous research, or investors managing cross-asset portfolios.

## Alphio AI: Best for Natural Language Trade Execution

Alphio AI is execution-focused. It integrates with Robinhood's MCP server and lets users place trades across stocks and crypto using natural language prompts — a genuinely different capability from a research tool.

Alphio is reactive rather than proactive. You prompt it; it acts. It doesn't schedule autonomous research agents and doesn't decode on-chain blockchain-layer data. If you want to execute trades through a conversational interface, it's built for that. If you want research delivered before you decide to trade, it's not the right fit.

**Best for:** Execution-focused investors who want to place trades using natural language.

**Not built for:** On-chain research, scheduled analysis delivery, or cross-asset intelligence.

## Robinhood Agentic Trading: Best as an Execution Layer, Not a Research Tool

Robinhood's agentic trading infrastructure is built on top of its 27.5 million funded customer base. It covers equities, options, and crypto execution with no additional charge beyond a standard account.

Worth being direct here: Robinhood Agentic Trading is not a research or analytics product. It generates no research insights, decodes no on-chain data, and provides no intelligence layer. It's execution infrastructure. Pairing it with a research platform makes sense. Treating it as a substitute for one doesn't.

**Best for:** Executing trades within the Robinhood ecosystem.

**Not built for:** Market research, on-chain analysis, or investment intelligence of any kind.

## Agenticly: Best for Scheduled AI Research Across Crypto and US Equities

[Agenticly](https://agenticly.app/) occupies a specific position that none of the platforms above cover: scheduled, no-code AI research agents that combine on-chain crypto intelligence for Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana with US equity market tracking — all inside a single mobile application.

The core difference is the delivery model. Instead of opening an app and running a query, you configure agents to run on a recurring schedule. Those agents analyze real-time market data across BTC, ETH, and SOL at the on-chain level, track US stock market movements, and generate personalized insights tailored to your portfolio. The completed analysis arrives automatically.

That matters most when markets move fast. When Bitcoin breaks a significant level or a major earnings report drops, having pre-configured research agents already running means you're not scrambling to pull data from three separate tools at once.

The app is available on iOS and Android, with a free tier to get started. The mobile-first design reflects how retail investors actually use research tools: on the go, between other tasks, not at a desktop terminal.

**Best for:** Self-directed retail investors managing both crypto and US equity positions who want proactive, scheduled research without stitching together multiple platforms.

**Not built for:** Institutional data science workflows, trade execution, or investors who only need raw on-chain metrics without interpretation.

**What does "on-chain analytics" actually mean for a retail investor?**

On-chain analytics refers to data pulled directly from a blockchain's public ledger: wallet flows, exchange inflows and outflows, miner activity, holder behavior. For Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana, this data can signal shifts in market positioning before they show up in price. Platforms like Glassnode and Nansen specialize in this data for institutional users; Agenticly decodes it for retail investors as part of a broader research package.

**What is the difference between a research app and an execution app?**

A research app delivers market intelligence and analysis to help you make investment decisions. An execution app places trades. Robinhood Agentic Trading and Alphio AI are execution tools. Glassnode, Nansen, Messari, AInvest, and Agenticly are research tools. These are different categories and shouldn't be treated as substitutes for each other.
