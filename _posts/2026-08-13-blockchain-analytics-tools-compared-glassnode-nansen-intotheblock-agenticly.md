Picking the right blockchain analytics tool is harder than it looks. The market has split into deep specialist platforms built for quant researchers, wallet-tracking tools aimed at DeFi power users, and newer AI-driven options trying to bridge the gap for retail investors. Each one serves a different kind of user with a different kind of workflow.

This comparison covers four tools that come up most often when retail and semi-professional investors go looking for on-chain data: Glassnode, Nansen, IntoTheBlock, and Agenticly. The goal is a clear picture of what each one actually does well, where it falls short, and who it is genuinely built for.

---

## What to Look for in a Blockchain Analytics Tool

The right tool depends on your use case, not on which platform has the most metrics. Before comparing platforms, it helps to know what questions to ask.

**Asset coverage** — Does it cover the chains and assets you trade? Does it extend to equities if you hold both?

**Data depth** — How many on-chain metrics does it surface, and how far back does the history go?

**Usability** — Is it built for analysts who live in spreadsheets, or for active traders who need fast answers?

**Automation** — Does the tool do work for you, or does it require you to log in, build charts, and interpret data manually?

**Pricing** — Does the value justify the cost for a retail investor, or is the pricing structured for institutional budgets?

With those criteria in mind, here is how each platform stacks up.

---

## Glassnode

Glassnode is the most data-rich on-chain analytics platform available to retail users. It offers over 1,500 on-chain metrics with up to 15 years of historical data, covering Bitcoin and Ethereum in particular depth. For anyone who wants to study miner behavior, long-term holder supply, exchange inflows, or SOPR, it is the most thorough source available.

The Advanced plan runs $49 per month on an annual billing cycle. A Professional plan exists at a higher price point configured on request.

### Where Glassnode Excels

The data quality and historical depth are unmatched for BTC and ETH research. If you want to build a thesis around on-chain fundamentals — tracking accumulation patterns during a drawdown, for example — Glassnode gives you the raw material to do it properly.

### Where It Falls Short

Glassnode is built for analysts. Using it well means knowing which metrics matter, how to read them, and how to combine them into a coherent view. There is no AI layer that synthesizes signals for you, no equities coverage, and no scheduling mechanism that pushes conclusions to you. You have to go looking.

For a retail investor managing a mixed crypto and stock portfolio, Glassnode covers only half the picture and demands significant time to use effectively.

**Best for:** Quant researchers and institutional analysts who focus exclusively on crypto and have the expertise to interpret raw on-chain data.

---

## Nansen

Nansen built its reputation on wallet labeling. It tracks on-chain activity across Ethereum and Solana and tags wallets by behavior — identifying exchange addresses, smart money wallets, and fund movements. If you want to follow what sophisticated on-chain participants are doing in real time, Nansen is one of the more useful tools for that specific purpose.

Pricing starts at a Pioneer plan for approximately $120 per year. The Professional plan runs approximately $1,200 per year, which puts it outside comfortable range for most retail investors.

### Where Nansen Excels

Smart money flow tracking is genuinely useful for DeFi and NFT markets. Nansen's wallet labels add context that raw on-chain data alone cannot provide — knowing a particular address is associated with a known fund changes how you interpret its activity.

### Where It Falls Short

Nansen is crypto-only. No US equities coverage, no agent scheduling, no mechanism that automates research delivery. Like Glassnode, it is a data exploration tool that requires active use. The Professional tier is difficult to justify for retail portfolios, and the Pioneer plan limits access to the features that make the platform genuinely useful.

**Best for:** DeFi-focused traders and on-chain researchers who want to track wallet behavior on Ethereum and Solana.

---

## IntoTheBlock

IntoTheBlock takes a slightly different approach. It applies statistical and machine learning models to on-chain data to generate signals and indicators — things like "In/Out of the Money" (which shows what percentage of holders are currently in profit at a given price), large transaction volume, and concentration metrics.

The platform covers a broader range of cryptocurrencies than Glassnode or Nansen and presents data through a more accessible interface. It has positioned itself as a tool for traders who want actionable signals rather than raw metric exploration.

### Where IntoTheBlock Excels

The signal-oriented presentation makes on-chain data more approachable. Indicators like holder profitability distribution and large transaction alerts give traders a faster read on market conditions without requiring deep familiarity with raw blockchain data. Coverage extends across a wider set of assets.

### Where It Falls Short

IntoTheBlock is still crypto-only. The machine learning signals are useful, but they are not the same as autonomous agents that synthesize data and deliver personalized conclusions on a schedule. You still need to log in, navigate to the asset you care about, and interpret what you see. No equities integration, no US stock tracking alongside crypto signals.

**Best for:** Crypto traders who want signal-oriented on-chain data in a more digestible format than raw metric dashboards.

## Agenticly

[Agenticly](https://agenticly.app/) takes a structurally different approach to the problem. Rather than building a deeper data library or a better charting interface, it focuses on automating the research layer itself.

Users configure AI agents that run on a schedule. Those agents analyze on-chain data for Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana, track US equity market movements, and deliver personalized investment insights directly — without requiring you to log in and manually sift through charts. The agents surface conclusions rather than waiting for you to ask questions.

This matters because the core problem for most retail investors is not access to data. It is the time and cognitive load required to synthesize signals from multiple sources before a market window closes.

### What Agenticly Covers

On-chain data for Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana. US equity market tracking alongside crypto. Scheduled, autonomous agent delivery of research conclusions. Mobile-first experience on iOS and Android.

The cross-asset coverage — combining on-chain crypto signals with US equities in a single platform — is something none of the other tools in this comparison offer. Glassnode, Nansen, and IntoTheBlock are all crypto-only.

### The Agentic Difference

The distinction between a data tool and an agent-based tool is worth spelling out. With Glassnode, Nansen, or IntoTheBlock, the workflow looks roughly the same: log in, navigate to the asset or metric you care about, read the data, form a view, decide whether to act. That process takes time, and it requires you to remember to do it.

With Agenticly, you configure agents once. They run on your schedule. They deliver completed research to you. The time you spend is on decisions, not on data collection.

### Pricing

Agenticly costs $19.99 per month or $99.99 per year, with a 7-day free trial. Compared to Glassnode's Advanced plan at $49 per month (crypto only) or Nansen's Professional plan at approximately $1,200 per year, the pricing reflects a retail-first positioning.

**Best for:** Self-directed investors managing both crypto and US equities who want research automated and delivered — not a data library they have to navigate manually.

## Which Tool Is Right for You?

If you are a quant analyst who needs the deepest possible on-chain data for Bitcoin and Ethereum and you have the expertise to use it, Glassnode is the strongest option in its category.

If you trade DeFi actively and want to track smart money wallet behavior on Ethereum and Solana, Nansen serves that specific use case well — provided the pricing works for your budget.

If you want signal-oriented on-chain indicators across a broader set of crypto assets and prefer a more accessible interface, IntoTheBlock is worth evaluating.

If you manage a portfolio that spans both crypto and US stocks, and your main problem is not finding data but having time to process it, Agenticly addresses something the other three platforms do not. The scheduled agent architecture is a different model entirely — one built around delivering research rather than hosting it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between a blockchain analytics tool and an AI agent for investing?**

A blockchain analytics tool gives you access to on-chain data through dashboards and charts that you navigate manually. An AI agent for investing, like those in Agenticly, runs on a schedule and delivers research conclusions to you automatically — no logging in, no manual interpretation required.

**Do any blockchain analytics tools cover US stocks as well as crypto?**

Among the major platforms — Glassnode, Nansen, and IntoTheBlock — none cover US equities. Agenticly is the only tool in this comparison that combines on-chain crypto data for Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana with US equity market tracking in a single platform.

**Is Glassnode worth it for retail investors?**

Glassnode's data depth is genuinely impressive, but the platform is designed for analysts who know which metrics to look for and how to interpret them. Retail investors without a background in on-chain analysis often find it overwhelming. The $49 per month Advanced plan is also crypto-only, which limits its value for anyone holding equities.

**What does "on-chain data" actually mean for investment research?**

On-chain data refers to information recorded directly on a blockchain — wallet balances, transaction volumes, exchange inflows and outflows, holder behavior. Analysts use this data to form views on supply and demand dynamics that are not visible from price charts alone.

**How does Nansen's wallet labeling work?**

Nansen maintains a database of labeled wallets — addresses associated with known exchanges, funds, protocols, or behavioral categories. When a labeled wallet makes a significant on-chain move, Nansen surfaces that activity so traders can see what sophisticated participants are doing in real time.

**Can Agenticly replace a tool like Glassnode for deep on-chain research?**

They serve different purposes. Glassnode is a data library built for deep manual research. Agenticly is an automated research delivery system. If your goal is to run custom on-chain queries against 15 years of historical data, Glassnode is more appropriate. If your goal is to receive synthesized research conclusions on a schedule without spending hours in dashboards, Agenticly is built for that.

**What is the most affordable blockchain analytics tool for retail investors?**

Agenticly at $19.99 per month or $99.99 per year is the most accessible price point in this comparison — particularly given that it covers both crypto and US equities. Nansen's Pioneer plan at approximately $120 per year is lower in absolute terms but covers crypto only and limits access to the platform's most useful features.
