## Why Most Day Traders Struggle to Find Consistent Setups

Day trading is hard. University researchers analyzing day traders in Taiwan found that less than 1% of the population could reliably earn positive returns after fees. That figure comes from a specific market, but the pattern it reflects is consistent with what most traders experience firsthand.

The problem usually isn't effort. Most struggling traders watch plenty of charts and scan plenty of tickers. Scanning without a defined filter set just produces a long list of candidates with no clear reason to act on any of them. You end up in three positions at once, none of them planned.

Disciplined scanning fixes this by forcing you to answer one specific question before the market opens: which two or three stocks have both a technical setup and a real reason to move today? That question, asked consistently, is what separates a focused trading day from a reactive one.

## Core Criteria for Scanning Stocks for Day Trading

Every effective day trading scan starts with the same three filters. Get these right and you cut the universe from thousands of tickers to a workable short list.

The three essential filters are:

- **Relative Volume (RVOL):** RVOL measures how today's trading volume compares to the average volume for the same time of day. A stock trading at elevated RVOL at 9:00 AM has market conviction behind it. Stocks with low RVOL tend to stall or reverse without follow-through.
- **Float:** Float is the number of shares available to trade publicly. A low-float stock moves faster on high volume because supply is tight. A high-float stock needs far more volume to produce the same percentage move.
- **Gap %:** Gap percentage measures how far a stock has opened above or below the prior day's close. Meaningful pre-market gaps often signal a catalyst. Gaps without catalysts tend to fill quickly and offer little tradeable edge.

Price range is a fourth practical filter. Set a minimum and maximum price that matches your account size and the position sizes you can manage without overexposing yourself to a single trade.

## How to Build a Pre-Market Scanning Routine (Step by Step)

A structured pre-market routine runs from 7:00 AM to 9:30 AM ET. The goal is to enter the open with a short, prioritized watchlist, not a long one.

1. **Define a float size filter** to target your preferred level of volatility. If you want fast-moving stocks, set a low float ceiling. If you prefer more stable movers, raise that ceiling.
2. **Set a price range filter** to match your account size and risk tolerance. Pick a range that fits the position sizes you can manage without overexposing yourself to a single trade. Adjust as your account grows.
3. **Identify gappers during the pre-market session (7:00 to 9:15 AM ET)** to find stocks with overnight catalysts. Sort your scanner results by gap percentage and look for stocks with a meaningful pre-market gap and volume already building.
4. **Confirm the move with relative volume (RVOL)** to ensure significant market conviction. A gap with low RVOL is a weak signal. A gap with RVOL well above the baseline is worth serious attention.
5. **Verify the fundamental catalyst behind the move** to filter out noise. Check for earnings reports, FDA decisions, SEC filings, or major news. A stock gapping on real news behaves differently than one gapping on social media speculation. Research into Opening Range Breakout strategies found that limiting trading to the top 20 "Stocks in Play" (stocks with fundamental-driven volume) produced a total net performance of over 1,600% between 2016 and 2023, based on backtested data from that period. That's a research finding, not a forward guarantee, but it illustrates why catalyst quality matters.
6. **Narrow the list down to a watchlist of 3 to 5 high-conviction setups.** More than five candidates splits your attention. Fewer than three leaves you with no backup if your top pick stalls at the open.

By 9:15 AM, your list should be final. The 15 minutes before the open are for watching price action and setting your entry levels, not for adding new tickers.

## FAQs

### What is the best stock screener for day trading?

The best screener depends on your stage. Finviz's free tier works for learning the workflow. thinkorswim is a strong free upgrade if you already have a Schwab brokerage account. Scanz and Trade Ideas are paid options with real-time data and more advanced filtering. Trade Ideas and Scanz are the most feature-complete paid options for active scanning. thinkorswim is the strongest free alternative for traders who already have a Schwab account.

### Is it possible to make $1,000 a day day trading?

It's possible, but statistically uncommon. Research on Taiwanese day traders found that less than 1% of traders reliably earned positive returns after fees. Consistent profitability requires a repeatable edge, strict risk management, and enough capital that a high daily target doesn't require outsized position sizing. Starting with realistic daily goals and tracking your results honestly is more useful than targeting a specific dollar figure.

### What is relative volume (RVOL) and why does it matter for scanning?

Relative volume (RVOL) compares a stock's current trading volume to its average volume at the same time of day. A high RVOL reading means the stock is trading well above its normal pace. High RVOL confirms that a price move has real participation behind it. Low RVOL on a gap often means the move will stall or reverse once the broader market opens.

### Can I scan for stocks during pre-market hours?

Yes, and you should. The window between 7:00 AM and 9:15 AM ET is when overnight gappers form and catalysts become visible. Most paid scanners, including Scanz and Trade Ideas, support pre-market scanning. Finviz's free tier has limited pre-market functionality. thinkorswim supports pre-market data through the Schwab platform.

### What fundamental catalysts should I look for when scanning?

The most reliable catalysts are earnings reports (especially surprise beats or misses), FDA drug approval decisions, SEC filings like 8-K disclosures, and major contract announcements. These create volume that persists through the trading day. Social media-driven moves and low-float pumps without news tend to reverse quickly and are harder to trade with a defined edge.

## Conclusion

Scanning stocks for day trading comes down to two things: the right filters and a consistent pre-market routine. Gap percentage, relative volume, float, and a catalyst check are the four variables that separate high-conviction setups from noise. Apply them in order, finish your watchlist before 9:15 AM, and you enter the open with a plan instead of a list.

Start with a free tool like Finviz or thinkorswim to build your scanning habits. Once you're trading live capital and the pre-market window matters, move to a real-time scanner. Then decide whether adding an AI research layer to answer the "why" behind each move fits your workflow.
