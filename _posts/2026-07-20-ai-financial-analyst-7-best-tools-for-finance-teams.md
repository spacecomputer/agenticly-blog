An AI financial analyst is software that supports research, modeling, and reporting tasks. The options covered here are ChatGPT for Financial Services, Claude, Gemini Enterprise, Hebbia, and Agenticly, an agent platform that watches markets for you. They speed up work but still need human review and verified data.

You open a 200-page filing at 4 p.m. and the memo is due at 9 a.m. That's not a reading problem. That's a bandwidth problem, and it's the one these tools are built to solve.

Picking the right one depends less on brand and more on type. Some tools are general-purpose assistants. Some read documents at scale. Others watch markets for you while you work on something else.

## What Can an AI Financial Analyst Actually Do Today?

An AI financial analyst drafts research notes, builds and updates models, summarizes filings, and flags changes in market data. It does the first pass, and you decide what to do with it.

Adoption is already well past the experiment stage. A survey of 244 financial services compliance leaders found that 71% of firms now formally use AI, a 20 percentage point increase over the previous year (ACA Group, 2025). Accounting looks different: a global survey of over 500 accounting professionals found that only 13% currently use AI for research and financial analysis (Karbon, 2025). Treat those as two separate populations, not one trend.

The time savings are real. In a survey of 529 finance professionals, nearly 63% reported saving over six hours per week using AI for research and analysis (CFOtech US, 2026). A KX survey of over 2,000 capital markets professionals found that quants achieve a 62% productivity gain when using AI.

Better returns are another matter. A Mercer study of 131 asset managers found that while 55% had integrated AI into an investment process, just 8% saw a resulting improvement in returns (AllMind, 2026).

The tools save hours. They don't promise alpha. Treat them as assistants to analysts, not replacements, and keep a person on every conclusion that moves money.

## 1 and 2: ChatGPT for Financial Services and Claude

**ChatGPT for Financial Services** targets investment banking and equity research. It uses GPT-6 Astra to build financial models, run company research, and generate cited investment memos. The data feeds are native: LSEG, Daloopa, PitchBook, and Crunchbase come built in.

The detail that matters most for audit trails is traceability. It lets you trace figures back to original tables in source filings. It targets investment banking and equity research, so it fits those workflows better than general bookkeeping.

The catch: it's strongest where those specific data providers cover your coverage universe. Outside them, you're back to checking sources yourself.

**Claude** summarizes lengthy financial statements and handles narrative-driven scenario planning. Finance teams favor it for context-aware reporting and clear explanations of budget variances.

It handles very large context windows, which helps when you load several documents at once. It also offers Excel, PowerPoint, and Word add-ins, so output lands where your team already works.

The limit is data. The profile lists no native premium market feeds like the ones above, so plan to bring your own documents and figures.

Best for ChatGPT: analysts who want models and cited memos with data included. Best for Claude: teams turning dense statements into readable variance and scenario write-ups.

## 3 and 4: Gemini Enterprise and Hebbia for Document-Heavy Work

**Gemini Enterprise for Financial Services** is an agentic platform for capital markets teams: intranet searches, data analysis, and custom agents built on Google's Argon and Carbon models. It connects natively to Docs, Sheets, and Gmail.

Prebuilt connectors reach SharePoint, Jira, and ServiceNow, and the multimodal search interface respects permissions, so people see only what they're cleared to see. That matters when internal knowledge is scattered across five systems.

The catch: it rewards teams willing to build and maintain agents. If you want a finished research memo with premium data attached, ChatGPT is the closer fit.

**Hebbia** processes large amounts of private and public documents, extracts information, populates models, and runs cross-document comparable analysis. Deal teams at investment banks and asset managers commonly use it.

Every answer cites back to the original source documents. Hebbia also uses Zero Data Retention, so customer data isn't used for model training. Pricing isn't publicly listed.

For non-public deal documents, ask every vendor, Hebbia and Gemini included, how they handle retention, access controls, and training. Don't assume. Get the answers in writing.

Best for Gemini Enterprise: Workspace-centric teams that want custom internal agents. Best for Hebbia: M&A and diligence teams comparing terms across hundreds of documents.

## Can You Use Free AI Tools for Financial Statement Analysis?

Yes, with limits. You can paste or upload a statement into a free tier of a general assistant and get a usable first read. What you won't get is the deeper workflow: premium data feeds, enterprise connectors, or audit-grade source tracing.

Here's what free routes handle reasonably well:

- **Plain-language summaries** of an income statement, balance sheet, or cash flow statement
- **Ratio walkthroughs** where you supply the figures and ask for liquidity, margin, or leverage explained
- **Variance commentary** drafted from numbers you provide
- **Question lists** to take into a management call

And what they handle poorly:

- **Long filings.** Free tiers cap how much you can load, so a full 10-K may not fit
- **Live data.** Without feeds, figures come from what you paste in, or from the model's memory, which can be stale
- **Arithmetic you haven't checked.** Models can slip on multi-step calculations

Check every number against the source. Never paste confidential client data into a free consumer account. Treat the output as a draft.

Paid versions of ChatGPT and Claude may offer more room and tooling, but they still don't replace a review step. Free tools suit learning and quick looks. Anything that reaches a client or a filing needs a human on it.

## What Are the Risks of Using AI in Finance, and How Do You Choose?

The main risks are bad data, missing citations, weak security, and over-reliance on output nobody checked. Match the tool to the work you do, then test it on a task where you already know the answer.

Start with the risks:

- **Data quality.** A model that guesses a figure sounds as sure as one that read it. Native feeds or cited filings reduce that risk.
- **Citations.** If you can't trace a number back to its source, you can't defend it to a client or an auditor.
- **Security.** Ask each vendor how it handles retention, access controls, and model training before any non-public document goes in. No tool here comes with security claims you should accept without asking.
- **Over-reliance.** Faster output isn't better output, so check results before you act on them.

Adoption data adds a warning. Where adoption is high, such as the 71% of surveyed compliance firms that formally use AI (ACA Group, 2025), governance has to keep pace. Where it's low, such as the 13% of surveyed accountants using AI for research and analysis (Karbon, 2025), the barrier is often trust and process, not access.

Then run a short checklist:

1. **Match the type to the task.** Documents point to Hebbia, models with premium data to ChatGPT, market monitoring to an agent.
2. **Test on a known answer.** Check outputs against figures you've already verified.
3. **Confirm citations and data sources.** Demand traceability.
4. **Put review in the workflow.** A named person signs off on anything that leaves the team.

## FAQs

### Will AI replace financial analysts or just assist them?

It assists. The evidence shows time savings, but improved returns are rare: only 8% of 131 asset managers saw them, even though 55% had integrated AI. Judgment, accountability, and client trust still sit with people.

### Is there a free AI tool for financial statement analysis online?

Free tiers of general assistants can summarize statements, walk through ratios, and draft variance commentary from figures you paste in. They cap file size, lack live data feeds, and can slip on arithmetic. Use them for learning and quick looks, and verify every number before it goes anywhere.

### What do people on Reddit say about using AI as a financial analyst?

Community threads tend to repeat three points: check every figure against the source, keep confidential data out of consumer accounts, and treat output as a first draft. Users like the speed on summaries and the explanations. They don't trust unverified calculations or invented figures.

### What are the biggest risks of using AI in finance?

The biggest risks are unreliable data, numbers without traceable citations, weak handling of confidential documents, and over-reliance on unchecked output. Productivity gains and performance gains are separate questions, so measure each one on its own.

### How do large banks use AI for research and deal work?

The sources here describe deal teams at investment banks and asset managers commonly using Hebbia for cross-document analysis, and ChatGPT for Financial Services targets investment banking and equity research. They don't document any specific bank's setup, so confirm how any institution uses these tools before assuming.

### Can AI handle non-public deal documents securely?

Hebbia states it uses Zero Data Retention, so customer data isn't used for model training. No verified security certifications or protocols are available for the other tools here. Ask each vendor about retention, access controls, and training in writing before uploading anything non-public.
