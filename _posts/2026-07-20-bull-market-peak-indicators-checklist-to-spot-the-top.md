You watch your portfolio climb for months. Every dip gets bought, every headline turns bullish, and a quiet question starts to nag: is this the top? You can't answer it by staring at a price chart.

That's not a conviction problem. That's a checklist problem. This guide gives you four layers to work through in order: on-chain, technical, valuation, and sentiment. Each takes a few minutes to read, and together they tell you more than any single number.

## What Does a Bull Market Peak Actually Look Like?

A bull market peak is a process, not a single day. Prices stall, rally, stall again, and the warning signs pile up well before the final record high. In the 2007 US stock market, the stretch from the first signal to the last record high lasted 18 months, an 18-month window. Sell on the first warning and you leave gains behind. Ignore every warning and you ride the whole crash down.

That's why no single indicator calls a top. Each one measures a different thing: what holders are doing on the network, how price is behaving against its own history, how expensive the market is, and how the crowd feels. Any one of them can flash early or stay quiet late.

The fix is to stack them. You check four layers, note which ones are flashing, and act only when several agree.

This matters most in crypto, where a bull run is a sustained stretch of rising prices driven by growing demand and rising confidence. Those runs end the way equity rallies do: euphoria, heavy retail participation, and prices that outrun any reasonable measure of value. The sentiment layer in Step 4 picks up that last stage.

Start with the layer that's hardest to fake: what's happening on the blockchain itself.

## Step 1: Which On-Chain Bull Market Peak Indicators Matter Most?

On-chain data is the record of wallet flows, transaction volumes, and network activity. It shows what holders are doing beneath the price chart. Two of the most useful bull market peak indicators in this layer are the MVRV Z-Score and the AHR999 index.

**MVRV Z-Score.** This compares Bitcoin's market value to its realized value (the price at which each coin last moved), then normalizes the gap by standard deviation. In plain terms, it asks how far the market has stretched above what holders collectively paid. The MVRV Z-Score signals extreme euphoria and a potential peak when it rises above 7.5. Keep one caveat in mind: provider implementation and the denominator history can shift readings, so check the same source each time.

**AHR999 index.** This measures the ratio of current price to Bitcoin's 200-day dollar-cost-averaging cost and to a modeled price, which sorts the market into accumulation and reduction zones. A reading of 4 or higher (≥4) suggests a bull market top. Treat it as a prompt to review your positions, not an order to sell everything.

Here's how to work this step:

1. Open an on-chain dashboard that tracks both metrics.
2. Note the current MVRV Z-Score and compare it to the 7.5 line.
3. Note the current AHR999 reading and compare it to 4.
4. Record whether the layer is flashing (either metric past its line), warming up, or quiet.

NUPL (net unrealized profit/loss) is another popular on-chain gauge. It tracks how much of the market sits on paper profit, and a crowd that's almost entirely in profit is a classic late-stage condition. Use it as supporting context, not a hard line.

If this layer is quiet, the top is probably not here yet. If it's flashing, move on to price behavior.

## Step 2: How Do Technical Indicators Like the Pi Cycle Top Signal a Peak?

Technical indicators read price against its own history. The most cited one for Bitcoin cycle peaks is the Pi Cycle Top, which watches two moving averages and flags the moment they cross.

**Pi Cycle Top.** It uses the crossover of the 111-day moving average and two times the 350-day moving average. A moving average smooths daily price swings into a single line. When the faster 111-day line rises through the slower, doubled 350-day line, the market has run far ahead of its long-term trend. That crossover is the signal.

The Pi Cycle has limits. Its historical timing can look better in hindsight than it works as a prediction, and a crossover is not definitive proof of a final high. Treat it as one vote, not the verdict.

**Moving averages and RSI as confirmation.** You can pair the Pi Cycle with two simpler checks:

- **Distance from the 200-day moving average:** price far above this long-term line means the trend is stretched
- **Relative Strength Index (RSI):** a momentum reading that climbs toward its upper range when buying is overheated

Neither has a magic threshold. Use them to confirm a signal you've already seen, not to start one.

To work this step, check whether the two Pi Cycle lines have crossed. Then note whether price sits well above its long-term average and whether RSI looks overheated. Mark the technical layer as flashing, warming up, or quiet.

## Step 3: Check Equity Valuation Ratios Like Market Cap to GDP

Valuation ratios tell you how expensive a market is relative to the economy or to earnings. They apply to US equities, not Bitcoin, so use this layer when you're judging a stock market top. Three ratios do most of the work: market cap to GDP, price-to-earnings (P/E), and the Shiller CAPE ratio.

**Market cap to GDP.** This divides the total value of the stock market by the size of the economy. CME Group research indicates the S&P 500's market capitalization reached 212% of GDP, the highest level recorded since 1929. That's a vendor-reported figure, and it's context, not a trigger. It tells you the market is richly priced. It doesn't tell you the date.

**P/E.** The price-to-earnings ratio shows what you pay for each dollar of profit. A rising P/E means investors are paying more for the same earnings, which is how a rally runs ahead of fundamentals.

**Shiller CAPE.** This version of P/E averages earnings over ten years, smoothing out the business cycle. DailyTickers reports that the Shiller CAPE ratio has stayed above 25 for nine years consecutively starting in 2017. That's the catch with valuation: a stretched ratio can stay stretched for years.

Read this layer as a warning, not a timer. Here's how to work it:

1. Check where market cap to GDP sits relative to its own history.
2. Check whether P/E and CAPE are elevated.
3. Ask whether the ratios are climbing or merely holding at a high level.
4. Mark the valuation layer as flashing, warming up, or quiet.

A market can look expensive for a long time before it breaks. The valuation layer tells you how much room for error is left. The next layer tells you how the crowd is behaving.

## Step 4: What Does the Fear and Greed Index Say About Euphoria?

Sentiment is the last layer, and it confirms a late-stage market. The fear and greed index condenses market mood into a single score, running from extreme fear to extreme greed. When it sits deep in greed territory while prices keep climbing, the crowd is buying out of excitement, not analysis.

The index gauges crowd behavior, so use it as confirmation after the other layers, not before. A greedy reading in the first year of a rally means little. A greedy reading alongside a flashing on-chain layer and stretched valuations means far more.

Look for these euphoria markers beside the index:

- **Headlines:** mainstream coverage turns uniformly bullish, and "this time is different" arguments spread
- **Retail speculation:** first-time buyers pile into the riskiest assets, and leverage use climbs
- **Dismissed risk:** warnings get mocked and dips get bought without a second thought

The evidence on retail outcomes is sobering. SEBI data shows that 93% of individual F&O traders lost money between fiscal years 2022 and 2024. That figure comes from India's derivatives market, so don't read it as a universal rate. It does show what happens when speculation runs hot: most participants lose.

To work this step, check the current fear and greed reading and scan the headlines and social feeds for the markers above. Then mark the sentiment layer as flashing, warming up, or quiet.

That gives you four layers, each with a status. The next question is how many have to agree before you act.

## How Many Peak Signals Should Flash Before You Act?

Act when at least three of the four layers are flashing, and treat two as a reason to prepare, not to sell. The 2007 top took an 18-month window from first signal to final high, so early signals are warnings, not exit orders.

| Layers flashing | What it means | What to consider |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Normal late-cycle noise | Keep watching; no change |
| 2 | Warning stage | Review position sizes; set exit rules in advance |
| 3 | High-risk zone | Trim in stages; raise cash; rotate toward defensive sectors |
| 4 | Euphoria confirmed | Scale out further; protect gains |

**A worked example.** Say the MVRV Z-Score has crossed its peak threshold and the Pi Cycle lines have crossed, but valuation is mixed and sentiment is only warm. That's two layers, so you're in the warning stage. You write down your exit rules and wait. A week later, the fear and greed index pushes deep into greed. Now you have three layers, and you start trimming in stages.

**Crypto versus equities.** The signals behave differently by market. Crypto cycles tend to produce sharper, faster on-chain and technical flashes, which is why CoinGlass tracks them together. The 2007 equity top was slower and murkier: valuation warnings arrived early, and the final record high came much later, after an 18-month window. Don't expect a crypto-style signal in stocks, or the reverse.

**Defensive moves.** If your stock portfolio is heavy in tech, one option at this stage is to shift part of it into utilities, consumer staples, or healthcare, which tend to hold up better when growth stocks fall. That's a framework, not advice, and you decide what fits your situation.

The rule is simple: more layers agreeing means more conviction, and fewer means patience.

## FAQs

### Can you show me a chart of Bitcoin's market cycle?

A cycle chart plots Bitcoin's price against indicators like the Pi Cycle Top, AHR999, and MVRV Z-Score so you can see where past peaks formed. CoinGlass offers this view by aggregating 30 historical cycle indicators in one place. Look for the moments when several lines flashed together near prior highs, not one signal alone.

### What are bull runs in cryptocurrency?

A bull run is a sustained stretch of rising prices driven by growing demand and rising confidence. In crypto, bull runs often end with heavy retail participation, euphoric headlines, and prices that outrun any measure of value. Those late-stage traits are exactly what the four-layer checklist is built to catch before the turn.

### Is a bull run coming in 2026?

Nobody can date a bull run in advance, and this guide makes no price or timing predictions. What you can do is track the four layers and note how many are flashing at any point. A quiet checklist suggests the market isn't near a top. A flashing one is your cue to review your plan.

### Which indicator has been most accurate at calling past tops?

No single indicator has called every top. The Pi Cycle Top's timing can look better in hindsight than as a prediction, and a crossover is not definitive proof of a final high. The MVRV Z-Score and the AHR999 index (≥4) are widely used, but treat each as one vote.

### How often should I check peak indicators?

Weekly is enough for most self-directed investors, since tops form over months rather than hours. Check more often when two or more layers are already flashing, since early readings are warnings, not exit orders. Automating the checks removes the need to remember.

### Can a market stay overvalued for years before it falls?

Yes. A stretched valuation ratio can stay stretched for years, which is why it works as a warning, not a timing tool. Pair it with the on-chain, technical, and sentiment layers before you treat it as a reason to act.

## Conclusion

No single number tells you the top. A bull market peak shows up as several layers agreeing at once: on-chain metrics past their thresholds, a Pi Cycle crossover, stretched valuations, and greed deep in the sentiment gauge. Until three of the four agree, you're watching, not selling.

Before the next rally leg, write down your exit rules for the two-layer and three-layer stages, and decide which sources you'll check each week. Setting the rules while the market is calm is what keeps you from improvising when it isn't.
